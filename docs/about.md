# About Kawika

> A progressive, gamified web platform empowering every Filipino to learn Filipino Sign Language through micro quests and culturally grounded interactive challenges.

## The Problem

Filipino Sign Language (FSL) is the language of the Filipino Deaf community and was declared the national sign language of the Philippines under **Republic Act No. 11106** (*The Filipino Sign Language Act*, 2018). The law calls for FSL to be used in schools, courts, workplaces, broadcast media, and government services.

Even so, very few hearing Filipinos know FSL. Communication gaps show up in everyday places — at home, in classrooms, at clinics, at the barangay hall. Learning resources exist, but they are often:

- **Hard to find** or scattered across videos and printed materials
- **Not habit-forming** — easy to start, easy to abandon
- **Disconnected from daily life**, teaching isolated vocabulary rather than real situations
- **Confused with ASL**, even though FSL is its own language with its own signs and grammar

## Our Mission

**Make Filipino Sign Language approachable, habit-forming, and part of everyday Filipino life — so more hearing Filipinos can communicate with the Deaf community.**

## Our Approach

### 1. Micro Quests
Learning happens in short, focused sessions of a few minutes. Each quest covers one small, achievable goal — five greetings, the numbers 1–10, the signs for family members. Small wins build momentum.

### 2. Gamified Progression
Borrowing what makes games compelling:

- **XP and levels** that reflect real progress
- **Daily streaks** that reward consistency over cramming
- **Badges** for milestones and mastered themes
- **A journey map** that unlocks new themes as learners advance

### 3. Culturally Grounded Challenges
Signs are practiced inside situations Filipinos recognize:

| Theme          | Example Challenge                                     |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Pamilya        | Introduce your *Kuya*, *Ate*, *Lolo*, and *Lola*      |
| Sari-sari store| Ask for an item and its price                         |
| Commute        | Tell the jeepney driver where to stop                 |
| Fiesta         | Greet neighbors and talk about food                   |
| School         | Ask a classmate for help                              |
| Emergency      | Communicate basic needs and ask for help              |

### 4. Progressive Web App
Kawika is designed to work well on the devices Filipinos actually use — affordable Android phones and inconsistent mobile data — and can be installed to the home screen like an app.

## Our Principles

- **Deaf-led content.** FSL belongs to the Deaf community. Signs, videos, and cultural context should be created or reviewed by Deaf signers and qualified FSL interpreters.
- **FSL, not ASL.** Every sign taught must be Filipino Sign Language.
- **Respect for variation.** FSL has regional variations; where they exist, Kawika should acknowledge them instead of forcing a single "correct" sign.
- **Accessible by default.** The platform itself should be usable by Deaf and hard-of-hearing users, with no audio-only content.
- **Free to start.** Cost should not be the barrier to learning.

## Who It's For

- **Families and friends** of Deaf Filipinos
- **Students** and teachers, especially in inclusive education
- **Frontline workers** — health workers, public servants, service staff
- **Anyone** curious about learning the national sign language

## Roadmap

| ----- | ----- |
| **0 — Foundation** | Project scaffold (React + FastAPI), design system, core data models |
| **1 — MVP** | User accounts, first set of micro quests (greetings, alphabet, numbers), XP and streaks |
| **2 — Engagement** | Journey map, badges, sign library, review mode for weak signs |
| **3 — Culture** | Scenario-based challenges (sari-sari store, jeepney, fiesta), community-reviewed content |
| **4 — Reach** | Offline PWA support, performance for low-end devices, educator/classroom tools |
| **Future** | Exploring camera-based sign practice feedback |

## The Name

**Kawika** joins the Filipino prefix *ka-* (a companion or fellow, as in *kaibigan* or *kababayan*) with *wika* ("language"). A *kawika* is someone who shares your language. FSL is a full language in its own right, and every learner who picks it up becomes a *kawika* to the Deaf community.
